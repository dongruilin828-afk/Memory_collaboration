Here is the straightforward answer:

In the context of academic papers, a **“综述”** (Literature Review or Review Article) is a comprehensive survey, summary, and evaluation of existing research on a specific topic.

Unlike an original research paper (which presents new experimental data or primary research), a review paper does not create new data. Instead, it synthesizes what is already known.

### Key Purposes of a Review (综述)

* **Summarize Past Research:** It helps readers quickly understand the history, current state, and major milestones of a specific research field without having to read hundreds of individual papers.
* **Evaluate and Analyze:** It compares different methodologies, findings, and theoretical approaches across various studies, often pointing out contradictions or consensus.
* **Identify Gaps:** By mapping out what is already known, a good review highlights what is *missing* or unknown, pointing out directions for future research.

You will typically see "综述" in two forms:

1. **As a standalone paper:** An entire article dedicated to reviewing a field (e.g., a "Review Article" in a journal).
2. **As a section within a paper:** The "Literature Review" section at the beginning of a standard research paper, which sets the stage and provides background for the new research being presented.
